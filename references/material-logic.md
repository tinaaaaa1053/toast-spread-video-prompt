# Material Logic

Use this guide to make each object behave distinctively while still reaching a spreadable end state. Choose the closest family, then customize it with the object's real visual features. These are generative directions, not claims about real-world physics.

## Transformation matrix

| Object family | Intact cues | Breakdown sequence | Spread medium | Sound vocabulary | Common failure to prevent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cellular or membrane-based | cells, pores, chambers, thin walls | buckles → membranes split → interior oozes | syrup gel or jelly cream | soft collapse, membrane crackle, sticky squeeze | cells remain intact in the coating |
| Crisp pastry or confection | sugar shell, layers, airy crumb, filling | fine crackle → shell collapses → filling absorbs crumbs | fluffy cream or smooth frosting | delicate crack, creamy compression, light scrape | dry crumbs or shell pieces remain |
| Crystal, gemstone, glass-like | facets, internal fire, translucency, refraction | hairline fractures → luminous collapse → facets dissolve | iridescent gel or translucent glaze | crystalline ticks, silky release, sandy glide | dangerous-looking sharp shards remain |
| Metal, pearl, or polished mineral | cool sheen, roundness, metallic reflection | crisp stress lines → structure yields → luster flows into paste | pearlescent paste or ultra-thin metallic film | muted snap, dense compression, delicate friction | hard fragments, thick metallic mound |
| Miniature model or engineered object | recognizable silhouette, small components, surface detail | seams crack → structure folds → components dissolve together | themed cream, gel, or molten layer | material-specific crack, dense squeeze, toast scrape | detached parts survive or respawn |
| Soft, waxy, or clay-like | smooth body, pliable edges, matte or wax sheen | compresses → folds merge → body relaxes | creamy paste or butter-like layer | soft press, damp fold, smooth drag | sudden liquid teleportation |

## Color discipline

- Preserve the object's dominant base color plus one or two accent colors.
- Convert patterns into ribbons, bands, flashes, veins, or gradients that suit the object.
- Use “iridescent” only for objects with shifting hues or an explicitly magical treatment.
- Use “glowing” for emissive concepts; use “glossy” for reflected light. Do not treat them as synonyms.
- When several colors are present, say they remain clean and distinct while blending at their boundaries. Avoid an undifferentiated rainbow unless requested.

## Choosing the coating

- Pearls, metals, and dark polished objects usually benefit from a very thin film so the toast remains visible beneath the sheen.
- Pastries and soft objects support a smooth or fluffy opaque layer.
- Translucent stones, glass-like forms, honeycomb, and icy models support a jelly or glaze layer.
- Volcanic, solar, fiery, or molten concepts support a glossy, contained molten layer.
- Highly detailed models should carry only two or three signature motifs into the spread; attempting to preserve every component creates visual clutter.

## Sound construction

Build sound as a causal progression rather than a generic ASMR list:

1. breakdown sound from the intact structure: crystalline crack, sugar-shell crackle, papery collapse, muted snap, granular crunch;
2. release sound from the new medium: sticky squeeze, moist compression, silky ooze, dense gel press;
3. contact sound from the spread: gentle crisp scraping, sandy glide, tacky drag, delicate low-friction scrape.

Keep audio close, dry, and synchronized. Exclude speech, music, exaggerated impacts, and unrelated ambient sounds unless requested.

## Failure-directed repairs

When the user reports a failure, reinforce the cause in the relevant action sentence instead of appending a huge negative list.

- Leftover chunks: explicitly show every piece deforming under the blade and merging before the spreading pass.
- Jump cut during transformation: describe the intermediate states and repeat “visible in real time in the same continuous shot.”
- Uneven coating: specify low knife angle, constant pressure, full edge-to-edge contact, and the intended layer thickness.
- Knife reverses or makes extra passes: define a visible start edge and finish edge; say it never lifts, reverses, pauses, or restarts.
- Object identity disappears too early: add two signature appearance cues before the first press and carry one cue into the spread.
- Extra hands or objects: specify one placing hand that exits frame, one spreading hand, exactly one knife, and an unchanged object count.
- Muddy colors: reduce the palette and describe clean ribbons or separated gradients rather than complete mixing.
- Unsafe sharp debris: replace shattering with fine luminous cracking followed by soft surreal dissolution, leaving no shards.
